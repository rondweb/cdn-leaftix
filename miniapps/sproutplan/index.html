Understanding Seeding Density & Operational Margins

High-yield microgreens production depends on strict spatial density parameters. Oversowing creates overcrowding, leading to restricted airflow and mold outbreaks, while undersowing compromises total tray yields.

1. The Blackout Phase

Placing a stacked weight (like concrete blocks or loaded trays) over sown seeds for the initial 3–4 days triggers root system expansion. This forces sprouts to grow uniform, elongated, and highly durable stems.

2. Density Calculations

Standard 1020 trays have approximately 200 square inches of grow area. Small seeds (Amaranth, Broccoli) require lower seeding weights, while large seeds (Sunflower, Peas) have high-volume hulls and need heavy densities.

3. Reverse Crop Schedules

To supply consistent batches to weekly markets, calculate crop cycles backwards. Sunflower and pea trays require early starts (~10-12 days), while radish and broccoli can be started later in the week.
